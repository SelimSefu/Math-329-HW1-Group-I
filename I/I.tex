\section*{Question 1}

Let \(s_i=1-2y_i\). For \(\lambda>0\), the objective function is
\[
f_\lambda(\theta)
=
\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{m}
\phi\!\left(s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\theta\rangle\right)}_{f(\theta)}
+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta\|^2,
\qquad \theta\in E.
\]
We first show that \(f\) is convex. The smoothed hinge loss \(\phi\) satisfies
\[
\phi'(z)=
\begin{cases}
0, & z\leq -1,\\
1+z, & -1<z<0,\\
1, & z\geq 0.
\end{cases}
\]
The values and piecewise derivatives agree
at \(z=-1\) and \(z=0\). Thus \(\phi\) is continuously differentiable.
Its derivative is nondecreasing, so for any \(a,b\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\phi(b)-\phi(a)-\phi'(a)(b-a)
=\int_a^b \bigl(\phi'(u)-\phi'(a)\bigr)\,du\geq 0.
\]
(The inequality holds for either ordering of \(a\) and \(b\).)
By Theorem~4.21 of the lecture notes, \(\phi\) is convex.

For any \(\theta,\eta\in E\) and \(t\in[0,1]\), linearity of the inner
product and convexity of \(\phi\) give
\begin{align*}
\phi\!\left(s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,(1-t)\theta+t\eta\rangle\right)
&=\phi\!\left((1-t)s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\theta\rangle
+t s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\eta\rangle\right)\\
&\leq (1-t)\phi\!\left(s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\theta\rangle\right)
+t\phi\!\left(s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\eta\rangle\right).
\end{align*}
Summing over \(i\) yields
\(f((1-t)\theta+t\eta)\leq(1-t)f(\theta)+tf(\eta)\);
hence \(f\) is convex.

Now
\[
f_\lambda(\theta)-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta\|^2=f(\theta)
\]
is convex. Since \(\lambda>0\), Definition~4.5 of the lecture notes therefore implies that \(f_\lambda\) is \(\lambda\)-strongly convex.
It is real valued on the whole Euclidean space \(E\), so Theorem~4.28 applies: \(f_\lambda\) has a unique global minimizer \(\theta^\star\).

Moreover, \(f_\lambda\) is convex and differentiable on \(E\).
Each loss term is a composition of the continuously differentiable
function \(\phi\) with a linear map, and the quadratic term is differentiable. By Corollary~4.23 of the lecture notes, a point is
a global minimizer if and only if its gradient vanishes. So, what we can say about the critical points is that
\[
\{\theta\in E:\nabla f_\lambda(\theta)=0\}=\{\theta^\star\}.
\]

\section*{Question 2}

We now set \(\lambda=0.005\). Write
\(g_i(\theta)=s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\theta\rangle\).
Each \(g_i:E\to\mathbb R\) is linear, hence differentiable, and
\(\phi\) is continuously differentiable as shown in Question 1.
Thus \(f=\sum_{i=1}^m\phi\circ g_i\) is differentiable.
By additivity of the differential and the chain rule, for any \(h\in E\),
\[
Df(\theta)[h]
=\sum_{i=1}^m \phi'(g_i(\theta))\,Dg_i(\theta)[h].
\]
Using the limit expression for the differential of \(g_i\), we obtain
\[
Dg_i(\theta)[h]
=\lim_{t\to0}\frac{g_i(\theta+th)-g_i(\theta)}{t}
=\lim_{t\to0}\frac{t s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,h\rangle}{t}
=s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,h\rangle.
\]
Substituting and using bilinearity of the inner product gives
\begin{align*}
Df(\theta)[h]
&=\sum_{i=1}^m
s_i\phi'\!\left(s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\theta\rangle\right)
\langle\widetilde{x}_i,h\rangle\\
&=\left\langle
\sum_{i=1}^m s_i\phi'\!\left(s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\theta\rangle\right)
\widetilde{x}_i,\ h
\right\rangle.
\end{align*}

The quadratic term \(r(\theta)=\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta\|^2\) is also differentiable. Indeed,
\[
r(\theta+h)-r(\theta)-\lambda\langle\theta,h\rangle
=\frac{\lambda}{2}\|h\|^2=o(\|h\|),
\]
so \(Dr(\theta)[h]=\lambda\langle\theta,h\rangle\). Consequently, \(f_\lambda=f+r\) is differentiable and
\[
Df_\lambda(\theta)[h]
=\left\langle
\sum_{i=1}^m s_i\phi'\!\left(s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\theta\rangle\right)
\widetilde{x}_i+\lambda\theta,\ h
\right\rangle.
\]
By Definition~2.18 of the lecture notes,
\(Df_\lambda(\theta)[h]=\langle\nabla f_\lambda(\theta),h\rangle\).
Since this equality holds for every \(h\in E\), we conclude that
\[
\boxed{
\nabla f_\lambda(\theta)
=\sum_{i=1}^m
s_i\phi'\!\left(s_i\langle\widetilde{x}_i,\theta\rangle\right)
\widetilde{x}_i+\lambda\theta.
}
\]
\section*{Question 6}

Consider the theoretically infinite sequence generated by gradient
descent with a fixed step size \(\alpha>0\):
\[
\theta_{k+1}
=
\theta_k-\alpha\nabla f_\lambda(\theta_k),
\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,
\]
starting from an arbitrary point \(\theta_0\in E\).

We check whether Theorem~4.31 of the lecture notes applies.

From Question 1, \(f_\lambda\) is \(\lambda\)-strongly convex.
Indeed,
\[
f_\lambda(\theta)-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta\|^2=f(\theta)
\]
is convex, so, by Definition~4.5 of the lecture notes,
\(f_\lambda\) is \(\lambda\)-strongly convex. Hence, in the notation
of Theorem~4.31, we may take
\[
\mu=\lambda.
\]
Since \(\lambda>0\), \(f_\lambda\) has a unique global minimizer,
which we denote by \(\theta^\star\).

Furthermore, let
\[
X=
\begin{bmatrix}
\widetilde{x}_1 & \cdots & \widetilde{x}_m
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
As stated in the homework, \(\nabla f_\lambda\) is Lipschitz continuous
with Lipschitz constant
\[
L_0=\sigma_{\max}(X)^2+\lambda,
\]
where \(\sigma_{\max}(X)\) denotes the largest singular value of \(X\).
Thus, for all \(\theta,\eta\in E\),
\[
\|\nabla f_\lambda(\theta)-\nabla f_\lambda(\eta)\|
\leq
L_0\|\theta-\eta\|.
\]

Therefore all the assumptions of Theorem~4.31 are satisfied with
\[
\mu=\lambda,
\qquad
L=L_0=\sigma_{\max}(X)^2+\lambda.
\]

For the step size prescribed by Theorem~4.31,
\[
\boxed{
\alpha=\frac{1}{L_0}
=
\frac{1}{\sigma_{\max}(X)^2+\lambda},
}
\]
gradient descent satisfies, for every \(k\geq0\),
\[
f_\lambda(\theta_k)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\leq
\left(
1-\frac{\lambda}{\sigma_{\max}(X)^2+\lambda}
\right)^k
\left(
f_\lambda(\theta_0)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\right).
\]
Hence \(f_\lambda(\theta_k)\) converges globally and at least linearly
to the optimal value \(f_\lambda(\theta^\star)\).

More generally, Theorem~4.31 can be applied to every fixed step size
\[
\boxed{
0<\alpha\leq
\frac{1}{\sigma_{\max}(X)^2+\lambda}.
}
\]
Indeed, if
\[
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{L_0},
\]
then \(1/\alpha\geq L_0\). Since a function whose gradient is
\(L_0\)-Lipschitz also has \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient for every
\(L\geq L_0\), we may apply Theorem~4.31 with
\[
L=\frac{1}{\alpha}.
\]
The resulting estimate is
\[
\boxed{
f_\lambda(\theta_k)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\leq
(1-\alpha\lambda)^k
\left(
f_\lambda(\theta_0)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\right).
}
\]

We can also obtain convergence of the iterates themselves.
Since \(f_\lambda\) is \(\lambda\)-strongly convex and
\(\theta^\star\) is its minimizer, we have
\(\nabla f_\lambda(\theta^\star)=0\). Hence strong convexity gives
\[
f_\lambda(\theta)
\geq
f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
+
\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta-\theta^\star\|^2.
\]
Therefore,
\[
\|\theta_k-\theta^\star\|^2
\leq
\frac{2}{\lambda}
\left(
f_\lambda(\theta_k)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\right),
\]
and using the estimate above,
\[
\|\theta_k-\theta^\star\|
\leq
\sqrt{
\frac{2}{\lambda}
\left(
f_\lambda(\theta_0)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\right)
}
(1-\alpha\lambda)^{k/2}.
\]
Thus the iterates converge globally and linearly to the unique
minimizer \(\theta^\star\).

For the value used in the homework,
\[
\lambda=0.005,
\]
the admissible step sizes certified by Theorem~4.31 are therefore
\[
\boxed{
0<\alpha\leq
\frac{1}{\sigma_{\max}(X)^2+0.005}.
}
\]

In particular, the largest step size directly certified using the
given Lipschitz constant is
\[
\boxed{
\alpha=
\frac{1}{\sigma_{\max}(X)^2+0.005}.
}
\]
\subsection*{Application to our implementation}

We now check whether the preceding convergence result applies to the
gradient descent method used in our implementation.

We use the fixed step size
\[
\alpha=\frac{1}{L},
\qquad
L=\sigma_{\max}(X)^2+\lambda.
\]
As verified above, \(f_\lambda\) is \(\lambda\)-strongly convex and
\(\nabla f_\lambda\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz continuous. Therefore, our
implemented gradient descent iteration
\[
\theta_{k+1}
=
\theta_k-\frac{1}{L}\nabla f_\lambda(\theta_k)
\]
is precisely in the setting of Theorem~4.31 of the lecture notes.
Hence the theoretical convergence result applies to our algorithm.

In particular, if
\[
\kappa=\frac{L}{\lambda}
=
\frac{\sigma_{\max}(X)^2+\lambda}{\lambda},
\]
then Theorem~4.31 gives
\[
f_\lambda(\theta_k)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\leq
\left(1-\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)^k
\left(
f_\lambda(\theta_0)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\right).
\]

Moreover, Corollary~4.32 of the lecture notes gives the stronger
conclusions
\[
\|\theta_k-\theta^\star\|
\leq
\sqrt{
\frac{2}{\lambda}
\left(
f_\lambda(\theta_0)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\right)
}
\left(
\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\kappa}}
\right)^k
\]
and
\[
\|\nabla f_\lambda(\theta_k)\|
\leq
\sqrt{
2L
\left(
f_\lambda(\theta_0)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\right)
}
\left(
\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\kappa}}
\right)^k.
\]
Thus, theoretically, both the iterates and the norm of the gradient
converge at least linearly, with
\[
\|\nabla f_\lambda(\theta_k)\|\longrightarrow 0.
\]

\subsection*{Comparison with the finite run}

This qualitative prediction is consistent with the behavior observed
in our finite numerical run. The plot of
\(\|\nabla f_\lambda(\theta_k)\|\) shows a clear decrease as the
iteration number increases. The gradient norm decreases very rapidly
during the first iterations and then continues to decrease more slowly,
eventually reaching the prescribed stopping tolerance after
approximately \(2.7\times 10^4\) iterations.

This agrees qualitatively with Corollary~4.32, which predicts that the
gradient norm tends to zero at least linearly. In particular, the
corollary provides a geometric upper bound of the form
\[
\|\nabla f_\lambda(\theta_k)\|
\leq
Cq^k,
\qquad
q=
\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\kappa}}
=
\sqrt{1-\frac{\lambda}{L}}
<1,
\]
where
\[
C=
\sqrt{
2L\left(
f_\lambda(\theta_0)-f_\lambda(\theta^\star)
\right)
}.
\]

It is important that this is an upper bound and not a statement that
the gradient norm must decrease by exactly the same factor at every
iteration. Consequently, the changes of slope and the relatively flat
parts visible in the numerical plot do not contradict the theoretical
result. The theorem gives a worst-case global convergence guarantee,
whereas the actual convergence speed may vary substantially along the
trajectory.

Finally, our numerical run is finite and is terminated once
\[
\|\nabla f_\lambda(\theta_k)\|
\]
reaches the prescribed stopping tolerance. Therefore, the experiment
does not literally show convergence of the gradient norm to zero.
Rather, it shows the gradient norm decreasing until the stopping
criterion is met. This is consistent with the asymptotic prediction
of Corollary~4.32 for the theoretically infinite sequence.
